# Independent method and claims review

Review date: 2026-10-28 (Asia/Taipei). Scope: research positioning and the evidentiary strength of the cold-start results. I read final-test metrics only after the independently checked `artifacts/main_v2/test_lock.json` and completed test run. This review does not select hyperparameters.

## Position relative to prior work

The project's research question is useful, but few-response profiling is established. [EmbedLLM](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bf5e4b85d203481d6e37bd32d9600162-Abstract-Conference.html) learns model and question representations, while [SkillEval §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28767v1#S4.SS2) already freezes a text-conditioned item network, fits a Gaussian prior from training-model profiles, estimates an unseen model's profile from few answers, and measures error on separate test items. SkillEval also compares adaptive probes with random probes and a same-probe score-count baseline. [Active Evaluation Acquisition](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/li25bp.html) studies selecting limited evaluation examples and predicting the remaining outcomes. [ATLAS](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/li26fx.html) uses Fisher information to guide LLM evaluation. These sources rule out claims that cold-start profiles, empirical priors, few-item benchmark prediction, or Fisher-guided probing are new in themselves.

The defensible contribution is a separately implemented, audited study of a particular **convex cold-start decoder and score-aware acquisition rule** on a fixed historical response release. Its strict unseen-item boundary, matched predictor/probe comparisons, rank-zero control, within-benchmark response permutation, and paired training-composition study make the empirical answer more interpretable. The score-aware rule targets the local variance of *reference-pool benchmark means*; that is a design choice, not a theorem about final-test MAE or a claim of first Bayesian experimental design. The benchmark offsets are dataset coordinates, and the low-rank axes are not validated human skills.

SkillEval's skill taxonomy uses complete item-bank text, including test-item text, and calls that representation transductive in its own limitations. This project keeps final target features out of fitting and selection and reads them for prediction. That distinction is worth stating, but the datasets, model pools, and scoring conditions differ, so published SkillEval numbers cannot serve as a direct performance comparison.

## What the development result supports

The validation-only eight-configuration search selected one shared setting: width 128, learning rate 0.001, latent penalty 0.002, zero interaction warm-up, and cold-start checkpoint selection. At seed 11, averaged across 50/100/200 balanced-random budgets after separately choosing each prior, macro-MAE was 0.04919 for neural low rank, 0.05151 for rank zero, and 0.05339 for structured rank eight (`docs/reviews/pilot_model_diagnosis.md`; `reports/development/selection.json`). Thus the proposed structured residual did **not** add value over rank zero in this development cell. The neural low-rank control was better by 0.00232 in score proportion, or 0.232 percentage points, than rank zero. This was a selection-stage comparison on 13 validation models and one optimization seed, not an unbiased estimate of a final effect. The declared practical primary predictor was neural low rank; all three architectures were carried into the matched final comparison.

The retained structured checkpoint did train an item-varying interaction head, but its residual posterior variance stayed close to its prior at 100 probes. A learned feature and a successful optimizer do not by themselves establish useful score prediction. The predeclared permutation control tests whether the item alignment in revealed responses changes score predictions after benchmark totals are held fixed.

The subsequently locked three-seed **validation selection grid** (`artifacts/main_v2/validation_grid.csv` and `artifacts/main_v2/test_lock.json`) chose prior multipliers 0.3, 1, and 1 for neural low rank, rank zero, and structured rank eight respectively. Their mean balanced-random validation macro-MAE over the three budgets and three seeds is 0.04997, 0.05140, and 0.05176. Neural low rank remained the best validation choice, but its difference from rank zero was only 0.00143 in score proportion (0.143 percentage points); structured rank eight remained slightly worse than rank zero. All of these values reused the validation models that selected the shared architecture, so they do not quantify final-test benefit.

## Locked main-test result and practical value

The completed [main report](../../reports/main/RESULTS.md) evaluates 13 held-out models from five reviewed lineage groups, with three training/acquisition seeds and 3,370 held-out items from five benchmarks. Under the common balanced-random probe sets, the declared neural low-rank predictor's macro benchmark-score MAE is **6.258, 5.099, and 4.274 percentage points** at 50, 100, and 200 responses. The scalar logit-shift comparator, added and tuned during validation before the test lock, obtains 6.195, 5.611, and 4.780 points. Thus the neural model is slightly worse at 50 and better by 0.512 and 0.506 points at 100 and 200. The paired five-lineage bootstrap intervals for neural minus scalar cross zero at every budget. At 200 responses, neural beats scalar for nine of the 13 individual models and loses for four; the individual differences range from -2.491 to +2.153 points. This is evidence of useful prediction on this cohort, but **not decisive evidence that eight model-specific coordinates add value over one scalar coordinate**.

The neural predictor does show clearer paired gains over the same-probe smoothed domain mean: 0.512, 1.498, and 1.741 points lower MAE at 50/100/200, with the reported descriptive lineage intervals below zero. It is also better than question KNN at 100 and 200; the 50-response interval crosses zero. These comparisons support the value of a fitted text-conditioned decoder and cold-start adaptation as a practical predictor on this historical target. They do not isolate a benefit of vector dimension or establish a new method relative to published external systems, which were not rerun under this information boundary.

The full structured rank-eight model scores 6.827, 5.649, and 4.571 points; rank zero scores 6.773, 5.769, and 4.775. Neither shows a consistent substantial advantage over the other. The declared neural low-rank model beats both on point estimates at all three budgets, but its paired interval against rank zero crosses zero at every budget. Therefore the proposed global-plus-benchmark-plus-residual structure is **not validated as a useful improvement** in the main test. The predeclared within-benchmark permutation is more specific: permuting support labels while preserving each benchmark's response total worsens neural low-rank MAE by 0.188, 0.760, and 0.912 points at 50/100/200, with descriptive lineage intervals excluding zero. Rank zero is exactly invariant, as its design predicts. This supports the claim that the neural model uses some question-response alignment beyond benchmark totals. It does not by itself prove that this information beats the scalar decoder on macro score error. Structured rank eight's permuted point scores are slightly better than its unpermuted scores, adding no evidence for useful item alignment in that architecture.

For acquisition, global Fisher improves over global random by 0.410 and 0.481 points at 100 and 200 responses, with the reported paired lineage intervals below zero; its 50-response comparison does not support a win. Balanced Fisher has no clear benefit over balanced random, and balanced diversity is **worse** than balanced random by 1.201 points at 200 responses. Balanced Fisher also loses to global Fisher at 100. Thus the supported policy claim is narrow: the score-aware global Fisher rule helped at two budgets in this cohort, not that active, balanced, or diversity probes universally improve prediction. These are descriptive intervals over five lineage groups across many comparisons and budgets, with no multiplicity adjustment.

The saved 200-probe-path selector timing averages about 3.15 seconds for global Fisher and 1.67 seconds for balanced Fisher across the 13 test models, versus under 0.001 seconds for random selection, on the recorded RTX 5070 Ti run. These are offline selector timings, not time or monetary costs of collecting LLM responses. Since the dataset supplies historical labels rather than live queries, no end-to-end evaluation-speed or price saving is established.

Benchmark differences matter. For balanced-random neural low rank, TruthfulQA has the largest mean absolute score error at each budget (9.13, 7.12, 6.28 points) and is systematically underpredicted (mean signed error -8.90, -6.95, -5.06 points). The aggregate improvement does not remove this source-specific bias. Additional responses improve the neural balanced-random mean across these three budgets, but several other policy paths do not improve monotonically.

## Final-result acceptance and wording

The project can claim measured value on the retained historical subset at all stated response budgets relative to smoothed benchmark means, and at 100/200 responses relative to KNN. The evidence for added value of the eight-dimensional model representation over the scalar and rank-zero comparators is weaker because all of those paired lineage intervals cross zero. Item-level Brier/log loss can support response prediction, but cannot substitute for the primary score error. Describe the numerical representation differences as observed cohort differences, not a reliable population advantage.

Probe-policy value must be assessed **within the same predictor** and budget against the declared random or balanced-random contrast. A better score from a neural decoder with Fisher probes than a baseline with random probes would confound representation and acquisition. Fisher's local variance reduction is only a proxy: a lower acquisition score variance need not produce lower realized benchmark MAE. All negative policy results should remain visible.

The study's target is an offline score on deterministic representatives from five released benchmarks and 88 reviewed historical models. The complete-case filter depends on held-out-model row structure, though not correctness. The original evaluation conditions and full item identities are unresolved, and detected grouping cannot rule out all paraphrases or pretraining exposure. Accordingly, do not call these official complete-benchmark scores or promise the same error for a newly queried LLM. Family exclusion is a stress test of training composition with fixed target membership and training count; swapping relatives also swaps other model properties, so any difference is not a causal ancestry effect.

The completed [matched family study](../../reports/family/RESULTS.md) tests structured rank eight and rank zero under a different preset configuration and training count. It holds target models, Qwen validation models, item groups, probe IDs, and 40 training-model slots fixed within each family pair, while swapping some related training models for unrelated ones. At 200 probes, the excluded-minus-relatives-allowed macro-MAE gaps for structured rank eight are +0.611 points for Llama (eight targets), -0.041 for Mistral (six), and +0.334 for Yi (three). The corresponding rank-zero gaps are +0.008, -0.146, and +0.489 points. Positive gaps mean relatives helped; the mixed signs and small target counts support a **heterogeneous, descriptive training-composition sensitivity**, not a general claim that family exclusion always hurts. Equal weighting of these three family cases yields +0.301 points for structured rank eight and +0.117 for rank zero at 200 probes, but there are only three broad lineage units and no valid per-family lineage interval. The swap also changes other model attributes, so the differences do not identify ancestry causally.

This family study does not directly test the declared neural low-rank primary predictor, and its absolute errors should not be compared with the main run as a family-generalization gap. Any later neural low-rank family analysis added after this coverage issue was identified should be labeled exploratory, especially because two family target models appeared in the main architecture-development validation set.
